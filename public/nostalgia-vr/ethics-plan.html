Ethical explanation & detailed planed organizing plan

As Sora-Domenjó warns that VR should not be treated as an automatic “empathy machine”, using VR and Nostalgia to increase donations itself is often assumed to be more manipulative inherently than other kinds of charitable activities. So to solve this issue, we attempt to maximaize the effect of participants' reflection instead of letting instant feelings guide them to make the choices. The difference between reflection and manipulation is that, reflection gives the participant space to interpret their own feelings, while manipulation tries to control the participant's conclusion before they have time to think. My proposed UIUC Nostalgia VR is designed around the first model. The experience may be emotional, but it should not remove user agency. The participant is told what the project is, why it exists, and what choices they have. The charity action will try to be organized not disguised as a required part of the VR scene. Instead, it is presented as an option for complete understanding of the conditions of people in need far away after the participant has returned to the real-world setting.

The process would begin with check-in. A staff member or volunteer would confirm the reservation, explain the length of the experience, and give a consent reminder. The participant would be told that they may remove the headset or ask to stop at any time. Next, the participant would enter a brief VR scene. The exact scene could vary, but the tone would remain calm and reflective(refer to the video at the front-page). For example, a user might see a dorm room, a campus walkway, a dinner table, or a quiet study space. The goal is to suggest familiar emotional contexts rather than to recreate a specific person's private memory. After the scene, the participant would return the headset and receive time to decompress. Only then would the website or facilitator show options for donating, volunteering, or learning more about the partner organization.

The project also avoids exaggerated claims. Volunteer organizers shouldn't assume that VR automatically makes people generous, nor should it claim that one short experience can solve complex social problems. Instead, we just use VR as one possible tool for attention, understanding and empathy. We will guarantee that the charity partner will get transparent information, accountable donation links, and meaningful volunteer opportunities. With regard to this website, it serves to support the following functions: informed participation, reflective experience, voluntary response, and transparent follow-up.
