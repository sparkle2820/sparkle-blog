Why we conduct the UIUC Nostalgia VR charity experience

This page explains why nostalgia is used as the emotional base of the VR experience and how the project connects memory, empathy, and voluntary charity action.

Why build a nostalgia-based VR charity experience?

Nostalgia is the emotional base of this VR experience because the goal of this project is to help you participants build a meaningful connection with people who may seem distant from their everyday lives. Currently many charity campaigns depend heavily on posters, statistics, short conversations, or images of hardship. These methods truly can communicate information, but they often fail to make the issue feel personally close to a busy student walking across campus. Here, nostalgia offers a different path. In our project, it aims to translate unfamiliar experiences far away into familiar memory using advanced VR technology: the feeling of returning to a familiar place, remembering family, school, friendship, childhood, safety, or belonging. The method of using VR is also what UNICEF is testing, with remarkable results: 1/6 VR viewers made donations across 40 countries, which is twice the usual rate.

In my project, nostalgia does not mean simply showing old objects or romanticizing the past. It means using familiar emotional settings as a bridge between the participant's own memories and the life of someone in need. For example, a VR scene might begin in a dorm room, a classroom, a family dinner space, or a graduation-like setting. These places do not need to copy the participant's exact personal history. Their purpose is to activate memory-like emotions that many people can recognize: being cared for, learning with others, sharing food, missing home, or hoping for the future. Once that emotional connection is built, the VR story can gradually introduce the recipient's story and show how poverty, war, disaster, or lack of educational resources can interrupt similar hopes and experiences.

Compared to other emotional feelings like empathy and regret, nostalgia is a both strong and long-lasting social emotion. It often involves important people, meaningful places, and moments that remind us we are connected to others. When students remember what has made their own lives feel safe or valuable, they may become more able to understand why another person would also want to protect those same things. The point is not to say, "Their life is exactly like yours." as this would erase the real differences and simplify their struggle. Instead, the appropriate message is: "The things you remember and value are also the things this person is trying to protect, recover, or reach." In this way, my proposed nostalgia becomes a bridge between personal memory and moral concern.

For UIUC students, I sincerely hope you can simply participate in this incredible experience, or you can be one of us, volunteering to help us organize this project. Campus life is full of places that already carry memory. By using VR to connect these familiar emotional spaces with stories of people beyond campus, this project turns distance into memory and memory into responsibility. That is why nostalgia here is not just a theme of the experience; it is the foundation that allows the experience to move from passive awareness to active care, which other projects won't have.
