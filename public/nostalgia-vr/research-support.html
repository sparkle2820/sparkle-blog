Research Support

Research behind the UIUC Nostalgia VR project

This page summarizes the academic sources that support the use of VR, nostalgia, charitable reflection, and ethical design in this project.

Reserve a Time Back to Homepage

Peter Singer, “The Singer Solution to World Poverty”

Main finding: People often ignore suffering when it feels distant, anonymous, or disconnected from their everyday life.

How it supports our project: This supports the need for a VR charity experience that reduces emotional distance and helps UIUC students feel closer to people they may never meet.

Kandaurova & Lee, “The Effects of Virtual Reality (VR) on Charitable Giving”

Main finding: VR charity content can increase empathy, guilt, responsibility, and willingness to donate money or volunteer time compared with traditional media.

How it supports our project: This supports using VR instead of only posters, flyers, or written charity appeals, because VR can create a stronger sense of presence and emotional involvement.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-026-09247-7

Gugenishvili & Nyström, “Virtual Reality and Charitable Giving: The Influence of Space, Presence, and Attention”

Main finding: In an experiment comparing VR viewing with desktop computer viewing, VR participants reported significantly higher spatial presence, attention allocation, spatial situation understanding, empathy, immersion, and emotional intensity. However, the study also found that actual donation behavior did not significantly differ between the VR and desktop groups.

How it supports our project: This supports the use of VR as a medium for creating stronger attention, presence, and emotional involvement in a charity experience.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11266-024-00666-3

Fetterman et al., “Nostalgia in Virtual Reality”

Main finding: Virtual reality can effectively induce nostalgia and can create feelings of social connectedness and meaning.

How it supports our project: This supports our central design choice of using nostalgic VR scenes, such as dorm rooms, classrooms, family spaces, or campus-like environments, as the emotional base of the experience.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1007/s12144-026-09247-7

Huang, Benford, Spence, & Blake, “Exploring Effects of a Nostalgic Storytelling Virtual Reality Experience Beyond Hedonism”

Main finding: A nostalgic storytelling VR experience produced higher enjoyment, appreciation, social connectedness, and well-being than a neutral text-reading VR experience.

How it supports our project: This supports the idea that nostalgic VR should not only entertain users, but also create a meaningful reflective experience.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1089/cyber.2023.0183

Zhou et al., “Nostalgia: The Gift That Keeps on Giving”

Main finding: Nostalgia can increase empathy, charitable intentions, willingness to volunteer, and actual donation behavior.

How it supports our project: This supports using nostalgia as a bridge between personal memory and care for others, rather than relying only on guilt or shock.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1086/662199

Merchant, Ford, & Rose, “How Personal Nostalgia Influences Giving to Charity”

Main finding: Personal nostalgia can increase people’s positive attitudes toward charity and make them more willing to support charitable causes.

How it supports our project: This supports the use of nostalgia-based emotional design in a charity campaign.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2010.06.013

Sora-Domenjó, “Disrupting the ‘Empathy Machine’”

Main finding: VR should not automatically be treated as an “empathy machine”; immersive experiences can be ethically risky if they exaggerate emotion, hide persuasion, or pressure users.

How it supports our project: This indicates possible ethical issues that could happen here and supports our project’s ethics explanation.

Source: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.814565

Overall research conclusion

Main finding: Existing research suggests that VR can increase presence and empathy, nostalgia can increase social connectedness and charitable behavior, and ethical design is necessary when using immersive emotional media.

How it supports our project: Our UIUC Nostalgia VR project combines these findings by creating a short, voluntary, reflective VR experience that helps participants connect personal memory with social responsibility.
